BandwidthSwap

A bandwidth market where the payment is the proof.

Paper v0.2 draft · Pilot phase 0 · Oct 2026

Build a link where there isn't one. Get paid on receipts.

Bandwidth Swap is a protocol and a sixty-dollar kit for selling network capacity to your neighbors, and a treasury that buys capacity on links it wants to exist. Nothing is rewarded for being online. Only delivered bytes, signed for by the person who paid for them, earn anything.

What it is

A market for pipes, priced by what it costs to go around them

Most places have one way to get a connection and one company selling it. The company paid to run the line, so it is not in a hurry to let anyone else use it. Bandwidth Swap treats a connection as a product anyone can resell, one hop at a time, and pays the people who run the hops.

The original 2021 paper proposed a blockchain where nodes announced their own uptime and speed and were rewarded for it. That doesn't hold up: a node can say anything. The 2026 revision replaces self-reporting with receipts. A user pays per unit of data, each hop is paid only when the next hop confirms it got the data, and the settled payment is the proof that bandwidth was delivered. There is nothing to attest and nothing to fake.

The part of the design that is still original is the price. A hop's share of a route is set by the opportunity cost of routing around it. A link that is one of five parallel paths earns a fifth. A link that is the only way across a gap earns the whole thing. That is the incentive to build where nothing exists yet.

Mechanism

How a receipt works

A receipt is small. The paper's Figure 2 gives the whole format:

(payer NYM, hop NYM, hop index, merkle root, bytes, t_first, t_last, prev receipt sig)
signed by payer

About a hundred bytes. That matters for the kit, because a hundred bytes fits in a LoRa packet, so receipts keep moving when the internet doesn't.

Design constraints

Four rules that make it hard to cheat

Payment is the proof

The only party who can't be fooled about whether bytes arrived is the one who paid for them. So the payer's settled payment is the proof, and no validator ever has to measure a relay.

No reward may exceed its fee

If a relay could earn more for carrying a byte than the byte cost to generate, it would carry bytes to itself. Every subsidy is bounded by this rule.

The treasury is the first customer

The treasury doesn't hand out bonuses. It buys bandwidth on links it wants to exist, at a posted price for a posted term. Whoever builds the link gets paid on receipts the treasury signs.

Quality is priced, not proven

A slow relay settles fewer bytes per window and earns less. Speed proofs are required only where a subsidy is claimed, which is the one place a false claim would pay.

Hardware

The kit

Two radios, because physics splits the job. LoRa carries the control plane: receipts, liveness, announcements. It reaches for miles through trees at a few kilobits per second, which is useless for a web page and perfect for a hundred-byte receipt. A second radio carries the data. Which one depends on your terrain.

Instructions

Build phase 0 tonight

Phase 0 needs no radios. Three Pis on your home network that keep time, tunnel traffic, and sign receipts. Everything you do here survives unchanged when the radios arrive.

  1. Flash three cards

    In Raspberry Pi Imager choose Raspberry Pi OS Lite, 64-bit. Open the settings gear before writing and set a hostname per card, one username for all three, SSH enabled, your Wi-Fi, and your timezone.

    CardHostnameRole
    1bw-housegateway and payer, wired to the router
    2bw-forestrelay and future hotspot
    3bw-mintreceipt aggregator

    Use 2.5 A power supplies. Phone chargers under 2 A cause random reboots on a Pi 3, and you will spend an evening blaming the software.

  2. Boot and find them

    Power on, wait about ninety seconds, then from your computer:

    ssh pi@bw-house.local

    If .local names don't resolve, your router's device list shows three new addresses. On each Pi, update and reboot:

    sudo apt update && sudo apt full-upgrade -y && sudo reboot

  3. Time, tunnel, logs

    Every node needs three things, in this order.

    • Time. Receipts carry timestamps, so clocks must agree. Install chrony on all three; point bw-forest and bw-mint at bw-house, and bw-house at the internet. The protocol's clock tolerance is 5 seconds; a relay declines payers outside it.
    • WireGuard. The data plane. One tunnel, bw-house to bw-forest. On a home network it adds nothing, but it is the exact configuration the radio link will use.
    • Logs to RAM. Install log2ram or set journald to volatile storage so the SD cards live.

  4. Run the receipt daemon

    The first real code, and there is nothing to download for it. A small service on bw-house and bw-forest that, every hour, reads the byte count on the WireGuard interface, builds the receipt from the format above, signs it with an ed25519 key generated on first run, and sends it to bw-mint. The Mint verifies both signatures and appends the receipt to a ledger.

    Your first dispute test: make the two byte counts disagree and see what the Mint does about it. The pilot's definition of success is that a dispute resolves from the receipt data alone.

Plan

Roadmap

  • Phase 0one eveningThree Pis on the home network. Time, tunnel, receipts, one Mint. You pay yourself. Proves the receipt code and the clock rule.
  • Phase 1one weekendRadios in. House to forest over a 5 GHz or HaLow pair, receipts and liveness over LoRa. Unplug the house's internet mid-window and show settlement still closes.
  • Phase 2a monthA neighbor operates the ridge. The treasury posts its first purchase commitment: this much data a day across this link, at this price, for this long. Paid in dollars on receipts. First real dispute.
  • Phase 3openOpen-source the kit and the bounty board. Anyone can post a named link and fund it. Anyone can build it.
  • Phase 4north starLinks into places where the internet is forbidden. This is the right long-term target and the wrong next step: receipts prove delivery, not location, and paying for a node in a hostile jurisdiction needs a location proof that doesn't exist yet, legal review, and an incentive design that never makes a border crossing the thing being paid for.

Honesty

What we don't know yet

The revised paper fixed the proof problem and opened a set of new questions. These are the ones that decide whether the mechanism works at all. The full backlog has twenty-two, each with the options and a default so the build is never blocked.

  • The unrecoverable fee. "Wash traffic costs the fee" is only true if part of every fee is burned or leaves the colluding set. The fraction isn't set.
  • The subsidy bound. If a fraction b of each fee is burned and the treasury matches fees at multiple m, self-dealing stays unprofitable only when m is less than b. The treasury-as-customer model avoids this for bootstrapping. It returns the moment the treasury matches third-party fees.
  • Receipts under onion routing. A payer can't timestamp a hop it can't see. The pilot sidesteps this by showing every hop; the real design needs two receipt types.
  • Payment windows. Inside a window the relay extends credit. The pilot uses hourly prepaid windows; interactive traffic will need something finer.
  • Location. No receipt can prove where a node is. Every subsidy for building in a specific place inherits this problem.

Phase 0 and 1 answer the clock, window, and offline-settlement questions directly. The economic ones get a simulation alongside. The location question is unsolved and we say so.
