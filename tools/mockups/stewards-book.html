The Steward's book

The Network Steward is a rule, not a party. Five percent of every fee comes here, and it leaves only one way: as prepaid offers to buy bandwidth on links the network wants, paid out against settled receipts and refunded on shortfall. Nobody holds it, nobody votes on it, and no payer can get back more of it than it paid in.

  • f 5% of every fee
  • m match up to 4%
  • cap f − 1% per payer
  • program window 168 windows
  • first link $1.00 standing
  • domain earth · USD · bw-mint

Open offers

An offer pays whoever carries the route, against receipts, at the premium above the relay's posted price. Each card says who can collect it, and whether anyone on the lab can.

What is real here: the 41 windows, the bytes, the 4.19¢ paid and the 0.21¢ that would be the Steward's at five percent. What is worked by hand: the three offers, their sizes, the match and the payouts, computed by the paper's rule over those numbers. What the lab cannot do yet: collect the second-bridge offer, since it has no third place; that is the program working as written, not a fault.

At a street's scale the same page reads differently: a hundred homes buying a gigabyte a day at ten cents pay $10 a day for carriage, the Steward's share is fifty cents a day, and a sole bridge carrying it all would see a $3.50 bounty for its competitor posted every week.
